 + Short Range Forecast Discussion (Day ½-2½)
 Short Range Forecast Discussion
NWS Weather Prediction Center College Park MD
327 PM EDT Mon Sep 07 2026
Valid 00Z Tue Sep 08 2026 - 00Z Thu Sep 10 2026
...Severe thunderstorms and heavy rainfall expected across the Northern
Rockies and the Northern Plains today, spreading southeastward this week...
...Modest relief from the heat across the midsection of the country late
Wednesday; cooler air begins to moderate across the Northeast and Western
U.S....
...Scattered thunderstorms linger across the Southeast...
As a large high-pressure system brings fine and cool weather across the
Great Lakes and the Northeast this Labor Day, a low-pressure system is
spreading unsettled weather into the northern Rockies and the northern
Plains. This is in response to a deep upper-level low getting ready to
eject out of the northern Rockies toward southern Canada. Strong dynamics
associated with this weather system will bring heavy rainfall and
scattered severe thunderstorms across the Northern Rockies to the Northern
Plains as today progresses. The low-pressure system is forecast to
intensify and move into the southern prairies of Canada by tonight, but
the potent cold front trailing from the low center will bring a round of
strong to severe thunderstorms as the front sweeps through the northern
Plains today, reaching into the upper Midwest tonight. By Tuesday, the
southern portion of the front will lose some momentum, allowing showers
and thunderstorms to develop over the north-central Plains and advance
gradually into the Upper Midwest by Wednesday morning. A Slight Risk of
excessive rainfall remains in effect across Iowa Tuesday to early
Wednesday.
As the high pressure system continues to expand southward into the Eastern
U.S. and encounters the heat dome anchored over the country's midsection,
the frontal boundary will eventually stall before reaching the Gulf Coast.
This pattern will allow scattered thunderstorms to linger across the
Southeast, including the Sunshine State, through the next few days. Behind
the front, much of the Northeast and Mid-Atlantic will begin a gradual
warming trend towards more seasonable temperatures as the cool airmass
begins to moderate, with the exception of northern Maine as reinforcing
cold air arrives Wednesday morning.
Temperatures across the Western U.S. will follow a similar warming trend
beginning tomorrow as mid-level heights rise with the departure of the
lingering upper-low. East of the Rockies, some much needed relief from the
oppressive heat is expected late Wednesday with the arrival of the
trailing cold front from the Canadian cyclone. Until then, however,
daytime highs in the upper-90s and triple digits combined with record warm
temperatures will maintain dangerous heat for the next 24-48 hours over
the Plains and Middle Mississippi Valley. 
Asherman/Kong

 + Medium Range Forecast Discussion (Day 3-7)
 Extended Forecast Discussion
NWS Weather Prediction Center College Park MD
141 PM EDT Mon Sep 7 2026
Valid 12Z Thu Sep 10 2026 - 12Z Mon Sep 14 2026
...Prolonged late-summer heat wave lingers for the southern Plains
through next weekend...
...Overview...
An upper level trough will push across the Northeast late this
week, accompanied by a cold front that will bring precipitation
chances from the Northeast to the Mid-Mississippi Valley. Upper
level troughing will also extend into the Northwest late this week
and be reinforced by shortwave energy next weekend. This feature
will gradually push east through early next week, resulting in
unsettled weather from the Northwest to the Plains, with some
winter weather potential for the northern Rockies. Meanwhile, upper
level ridging will persist across the southern tier, maintaining
hot conditions across the south-central U.S.
...Guidance/Predictability Assessment...
Model guidance remains in good agreement on the synoptic scale
pattern through the end of the week, with relatively flat/zonal
upper level flow leading to some minor timing differences. Model
spread increases significantly next weekend into early next week as
the models struggle to resolve upper level interactions between
various lows and troughs extending from Canada into the northern
tier of the lower 48 states. These interactions will ultimately
determine the evolution of the upper level trough/shortwave in the
Northwest next weekend.
Agreement among model solutions has somewhat improved since the
overnight forecast, now reflecting the most likely solution to be
slower with less interaction with energy in Canada. The 06Z runs of
the AI-GFS and EC-AIFS highlighted this trend and were even slower
than the other deterministic solutions, though the 12Z AI-GFS
is a bit too dramatic with this trend. The GFS runs have remained
consistently too progressive, but the 12Z run showed a significant
slow down and falls closer to the consensus. Ensemble means also
showed a large spread of solutions, further highlighting the
uncertainty surrounding the Northwestern trough evolution. 
Given good agreement, a near even blend of the deterministic
guidance was used as a starting point for the forecast through the
end of this week. Ensemble means from the ECENS and GEFS were
added in increasing amounts through the weekend to help smooth out
model differences, and the GFS was phased out since it was too
progressive. This blend maintained continuity with the overnight
forecast and the National Blend of Models.
...Weather/Hazards Highlights...
The long duration heat wave is expected to linger over the
Mid/Lower Mississippi Valley and southern Plains through much of
the week with widespread Major to Extreme HeatRisk. Key Messages
remain in effect with several record minimum and maximum
temperatures in the forecast. The pattern becomes more uncertain
by the weekend, but there is some trend for relief to northern
areas, confining the greatest heat threat to the southern Plains.
Above normal heat may also build briefly across the Southwest and
parts of California mid-week as upper level ridging extends into
the region.
A low pressure system moving through eastern Canada will push a
cold front across the Midwest and Northeast, which will trigger
showers and thunderstorms from the Northeast and Mid-Atlantic to
the Ohio and Mississippi Valleys Wednesday into Thursday. Locally
heavy rainfall will be possible, especially in the Ohio Valley
where
the front will encounter an anomalously moist air mass and in the
central Appalachians where terrain may enhance precipitation
totals. This frontal boundary should move relatively quickly, which
will help to limit flash flood potential.
The focus for precipitation will shift to the Northwest late this
week into next weekend as upper level troughing strengthens. This
trough will push a cold front south across the Northwest and
northern Plains late this week, which will bring unsettled weather
to these regions. Early next week, the front will continue to move
south across the Great Basin and Intermountain West while low
pressure strengthens along the boundary over the northern/central
Plains. Wintry weather will be possible in the northern Rockies
next weekend as cooler air moves in behind the cold front, and
heavy rainfall potential may develop over eastern Plains into the
Upper Midwest ahead of the strengthening low early next week.
Daily rounds of diurnal showers and thunderstorms are expected to
continue in the Southeast and Southwest. A couple of weak frontal
boundaries will move into the Southeast and dissipate, which will
focus moisture and instability across the region. In the
Southwest, monsoonal moisture will continue focus showers and
thunderstorms in the Four Corners region.
Dolan/Santorelli

 + Hawaii Forecast Discussion (Day 1-7)
Hawaii Extended Forecast Discussion
NWS Weather Prediction Center College Park MD
335 AM EDT Mon Sep 7 2026
Valid 00Z Tue 08 Sep 2026 - 00Z Tue 15 Sep 2026
All eyes in Hawaii at the start of the period will be on Hurricane
Lowell as it tracks west of Niihau and Kauai, but close enough to
those islands to have hurricane and heavy rainfall concerns. The
higher elevations of Kauai will allow for enhanced rainfall into
the leeward/south side of the island as the outer rainbands of
Lowell move northward and the southerly flow associated therewith
brings heavy rainfall to the usually drier sides of those islands.
Flash flooding will be a notable concern. Enhanced easterly flow
as a result of the passage of the hurricane means the other
islands also get heavier rains, but for the most part will be
aimed at the windward and typically wetter sides of those islands.
Once Lowell moves away into the Papahanaumokuakea Marine National
Monument by Tuesday, a more typical trade wind weather regime will
return to the islands. A tropical wave may impact the islands 
late weekend/early next week, but the timing and intensity of that
wave remains to be seen in the guidance.
Wegman

Day 1
Valid 01Z Tue Sep 08 2026 - 12Z Tue Sep 08 2026
...THERE IS A MARGINAL RISK OF EXCESSIVE RAINFALL FOR A PORTION OF
NORTHWESTERN MONTANA, THE SOUTHWEST, AND THE UPPER MIDWEST...
...Northwest Montana...
The comma head/deformation precip band rotating around surface low
pressure in Canada will continue to weaken and pull away from the
region overnight. Recent rainfall rates have been modest, but FFG
values remain low and rain has fallen most of the day. We have
downgraded the Slight Risk to a Marginal for the overnight hours to
cover the lower chance of some flood issues.
...Upper Mississippi Valley...
The height falls ejecting northeastward from the Northern Rockies
into the Northern Plains will strengthen the low level southerly
flow along and ahead of the associated warm frontal boundary moving
through the Dakotas, raising PW values to 1.75-2.00"+. Overall
favorable large scale conditions will support organized pre-
frontal/frontal convection over the Upper MS Valley this
evening/overnight. Convection should be progressive which will
limit the excessive rainfall potential -- the Marginal risk
suffices.
...Southeastern Arizona into western to central New Mexico...
Ongoing isolated convection continues at 01Z within the rather
moist environment with PW values still above average. Cells will
remain rather isolated and eventually wane later this evening, but
will maintain the Marginal risk for the earlier hours.
...Areas in the Southeast/Gulf Coast...
Convection has diminished and we have removed the Marginal risk for
the overnight hours.
Fracasso/Oravec

...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF EXCESSIVE RAINFALL OVER IOWA...
2030Z Update...
...Florida Peninsula...
Anomalous PWs up to 2.25" and a stalled frontal boundary will
utilize 1,000-2,000 J/kg of MLCAPE to trigger more scattered
thunderstorms capable of producing 2-3"/hr rainfall rates. The
setup is not too different than the setup that is supporting the
Marginal Risk for Day 1, but this time HREF/REFS members show
locally heavy rainfall rates could reside along the western coast
of FL. Slow storm motions with the aforementioned rates mentioned
above suggest the potential for a localize flash flood threat
Tuesday afternoon.
...Midwest & Southwest Marginals...
The Marginal Risks in the Midwest and the Southwest were merged to
account for an increase in WPC QPF over eastern CO and far western
KS. PWs are likely to surpass 1.25", which is above the 97.5
climatological percentile per ECMWF guidance, and a nearby frontal
boundary will help to generate thunderstorms in an environment
containing 500-1,000 J/kg MUCAPE. The QPF also increased along the
cold front in the Central Plains and Midwest, supporting an
expansion of the Marginal in those areas.
Mullinax
---Previous Discussion---
...Upper Mississippi Valley into the Upper Lakes...
The surface front pushing east into the Northern Plains day 1 will
remain very progressive day 2 into the Upper Mississippi
Valley/Upper Lakes. The progressive nature of the front will
continue to be a detriment to widespread runoff issues. However,
the southwestern portion of this front will slow somewhat compared
to the farther northern portion in the vicinity of eastern NE
/northeast Kansas, east into Iowa. Across these regions there is a
better chance of a period of training of cells late Tuesday/early
hours of Wednesday. This is shown in the latest guidance that has
consensus for a heavier axis of qpf here. A small slight risk area
was added across portions of central to southern Iowa for this
training potential. RRFS neighborhood probabilities are fairly high
for 1 and 2"+ amounts across the slight risk area, with a smaller
50%+ probability for 3"+ amounts over east central Iowa.
Marginal risk areas
..Eastern Arizona into western to central New Mexico...
No significant changes day 2 to the axis of above average PW
values extending from eastern portions of Arizona into western to
central New Mexico. Continued low confidence with qpf details, but
potential for another round of widespread scattered convection
across this area. Locally heavy rain and isolated runoff issues
again possible. Anomalous PW values also expected to push farther
north from Southern CA into Central CA. At the moment, models are
not showing precip developing day 2 in this anomalous axis.
The previous marginal risk area over northeast Montana was removed
given latest model qpf consensus. The broad marginal was also
removed along the Gulf coast into FL given less organized precip
than day 1
Oravec

...THERE IS A MARGINAL RISK OF EXCESSIVE RAINFALL FROM THE SIERRA
INTO SOUTHEAST CALIFORNIA, THE SOUTHWEST, THE MID MISSISSIPPI
VALLEY INTO THE OHIO VALLEY AND NORTHERN NEW YORK STATE INTO
NORTHERN VERMONT...
2030Z Update...
The Marginals in the Southwest and Northeast contained minor
adjustments to account for latest trends in guidance and changes
in WPC QPF. No upgrades were made in those regions. While no Slight
Risk was issued this cycle, there was a notable bump in QPF over
parts of the Mid Mississippi and Ohio Valleys that led to some
consideration for a Slight Risk, particularly in southern MO.
Assessing model guidance showed the both QPF totals and the
location of QPF maxima remained unclear, and soils in southern MO
could use the rain given worsening drought conditions. We will
continue to monitor model trends closely as the ingredients for
scattered instances of flash flooding (anomalous PWs, ample
instability, potential training) will be available to developing
thunderstorms on Thursday.
Mullinax
---Previous Discussion---
...Sierra, Southeast California, Southwest...
A very broad area of anomalous PW values, 2 to 3+ standard
deviations above the mean, will stretch day 3 from central to
southern California, across the southern Great Basin and into the
Southwest. Latest models do show scattered precip in this high PW
axis from the Sierra into Southeast California and over the
Southwest. The heaviest totals continue across similar areas day 1
and 2 from eastern Arizona into western to central NM where a
marginal risk continues day 3. The marginal risk was expanded
northward from day 2 into the Sierra to capture any locally heavy
precip amounts in the much above average PW axis.
...Lower Missouri, Mid Mississippi, Ohio Valley region...
A surface cold front expected to sink southward through the Mid
MS, Lower MO and into the OH Valley region day 3. PW values
expected to remain anomalous along this front with values 2"+,
supporting potential for widespread moderate to locally heavy
totals. The front remains very progressive to the south, which
along with relatively high FFG values, will limit widespread runoff
potential.
...Northern NY State into Northern Vermont...
The northeastern portion of the front moving south into the Mid MS
Valley/OH Valley region will also remain very progressive to the
east across the eastern Lakes, northern NY State and northern New
England. PW values expected to be anomalous along this portion of
the front also, with values 1.75-2.00"+. No changes to the
marginal risk area for far northern NY state into northern VT where
model consensus is for moderate to heavy precip totals. The
expected very progressive nature of the front will be a detriment
to widespread runoff issues, but the anomalous PW values and
potential for a brief period of training may produce isolated
issues.
Oravec

Day 4 and Day 5
Valid 12Z Thu Sep 10 2026 - 12Z Sat Sep 12 2026
...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF EXCESSIVE RAINFALL FOR PORTIONS OF THE
CENTRAL APPALACHIANS AND UPPER OHIO VALLEY ON THURSDAY...
...Central Appalachians and Upper Ohio Valley...
A fast-moving cold front tracking east across the Midwest will
provide the forcing, coincident with a strong upper level shortwave
trough, for numerous showers and thunderstorms to impact the
central Appalachians and upper Ohio Valley Thursday afternoon and
evening. Ahead of this front, deep tropical moisture from off the
Gulf will advect northeastward into the region. PWATs are expected
to exceed 1.75 inches, perhaps exceeding 2 inches in spots, as the
thunderstorms rumble through. The front itself will be aligned
parallel to the mountains, moving southeastward/perpendicular to
the mountains. Thus, ideal uplift will be in place as the front
hits the mountains all at roughly the same time. The northwesterly
flow behind the front running straight into the mountains will
locally enhance the uplift, and thus strengthen the numerous storms
expected Thursday afternoon. Soil moisture levels are about normal
for this time of year and FFG values are only around 1"/hr
(currently). Fortunately, no significant rainfall is expected in
this area until Thursday, which should help mitigate some of the
resultant flash flooding as the storms move through. However, with
ideal uplift, there will likely be some training and backbuilding
as continued uplift through the afternoon and evening allows repeat
thunderstorms to develop on the windward sides of the mountains,
then continue advecting southeastward.
Given the abundant moisture and forcing present, as well as the
flash-flood-prone nature of many of the mountain and river valleys
of the central Appalachians, as well as any urban concerns from
Pittsburgh south through Charleston, the Slight Risk area was
maintained with this update. Model guidance is in good agreement
on the position of the front during this period, with the storms
moving through at peak heating...maximizing their rainfall output.
...Marginal Risks...
Across the Southeast and Florida Peninsula, rounds of afternoon
showers and thunderstorms are expected both Thursday and Friday as
moisture levels remain elevated and winds aloft are light. Typical
heavy rains are expected along the Gulf Coast. While any one storm
is unlikely to cause flash flooding, the widespread coverage of
storms and the decently high likelihood that storms will impact
more flood- sensitive urban areas led to the continuation of the
Marginal Risk for these areas.
The monsoon in the Southwest will be ongoing both Thursday and
Friday, but its coverage and intensity should be subdued compared
to recent days. Thus, the Marginal Risks are generally confined to
Arizona and New Mexico.
A strong shortwave will track along the Montana/Canada border
Thursday night into Friday, but most of the guidance indicates that
the heaviest rain will remain in Canada. In addition, little to no
instability should keep rates in check despite FFG values
0.75-1.25"/hr. Removed the Marginal risk for D5 with this update.
Fracasso/Wegman

the mountains. Thus, ideal uplift will be in place as the front
hits the mountains all at roughly the same time. The northwesterly
flow behind the front running straight into the mountains will
locally enhance the uplift, and thus strengthen the numerous storms
expected Thursday afternoon. Soil moisture levels are about normal
for this time of year and FFG values are only around 1"/hr
(currently). Fortunately, no significant rainfall is expected in
this area until Thursday, which should help mitigate some of the
resultant flash flooding as the storms move through. However, with
ideal uplift, there will likely be some training and backbuilding
as continued uplift through the afternoon and evening allows repeat
thunderstorms to develop on the windward sides of the mountains,
then continue advecting southeastward.
Given the abundant moisture and forcing present, as well as the
flash-flood-prone nature of many of the mountain and river valleys
of the central Appalachians, as well as any urban concerns from
Pittsburgh south through Charleston, the Slight Risk area was
maintained with this update. Model guidance is in good agreement
on the position of the front during this period, with the storms
moving through at peak heating...maximizing their rainfall output.
...Marginal Risks...
Across the Southeast and Florida Peninsula, rounds of afternoon
showers and thunderstorms are expected both Thursday and Friday as
moisture levels remain elevated and winds aloft are light. Typical
heavy rains are expected along the Gulf Coast. While any one storm
is unlikely to cause flash flooding, the widespread coverage of
storms and the decently high likelihood that storms will impact
more flood- sensitive urban areas led to the continuation of the
Marginal Risk for these areas.
The monsoon in the Southwest will be ongoing both Thursday and
Friday, but its coverage and intensity should be subdued compared
to recent days. Thus, the Marginal Risks are generally confined to
Arizona and New Mexico.
A strong shortwave will track along the Montana/Canada border
Thursday night into Friday, but most of the guidance indicates that
the heaviest rain will remain in Canada. In addition, little to no
instability should keep rates in check despite FFG values
0.75-1.25"/hr. Removed the Marginal risk for D5 with this update.
Fracasso/Wegman

...Prolonged late-summer heat wave lingers for the southern Plains
through next weekend...
...Overview...
An upper level trough will push across the Northeast late this
week, accompanied by a cold front that will bring precipitation
chances from the Northeast to the Mid-Mississippi Valley. Upper
level troughing will also extend into the Northwest late this week
and be reinforced by shortwave energy next weekend. This feature
will gradually push east through early next week, resulting in
unsettled weather from the Northwest to the Plains, with some
winter weather potential for the northern Rockies. Meanwhile, upper
level ridging will persist across the southern tier, maintaining
hot conditions across the south-central U.S.
...Guidance/Predictability Assessment...
Model guidance remains in good agreement on the synoptic scale
pattern through the end of the week, with relatively flat/zonal
upper level flow leading to some minor timing differences. Model
spread increases significantly next weekend into early next week as
the models struggle to resolve upper level interactions between
various lows and troughs extending from Canada into the northern
tier of the lower 48 states. These interactions will ultimately
determine the evolution of the upper level trough/shortwave in the
Northwest next weekend.
Agreement among model solutions has somewhat improved since the
overnight forecast, now reflecting the most likely solution to be
slower with less interaction with energy in Canada. The 06Z runs of
the AI-GFS and EC-AIFS highlighted this trend and were even slower
than the other deterministic solutions, though the 12Z AI-GFS
is a bit too dramatic with this trend. The GFS runs have remained
consistently too progressive, but the 12Z run showed a significant
slow down and falls closer to the consensus. Ensemble means also
showed a large spread of solutions, further highlighting the
uncertainty surrounding the Northwestern trough evolution. 
Given good agreement, a near even blend of the deterministic
guidance was used as a starting point for the forecast through the
end of this week. Ensemble means from the ECENS and GEFS were
added in increasing amounts through the weekend to help smooth out
model differences, and the GFS was phased out since it was too
progressive. This blend maintained continuity with the overnight
forecast and the National Blend of Models.
...Weather/Hazards Highlights...
The long duration heat wave is expected to linger over the
Mid/Lower Mississippi Valley and southern Plains through much of
the week with widespread Major to Extreme HeatRisk. Key Messages
remain in effect with several record minimum and maximum
temperatures in the forecast. The pattern becomes more uncertain
by the weekend, but there is some trend for relief to northern
areas, confining the greatest heat threat to the southern Plains.
Above normal heat may also build briefly across the Southwest and
parts of California mid-week as upper level ridging extends into
the region.
A low pressure system moving through eastern Canada will push a
cold front across the Midwest and Northeast, which will trigger
showers and thunderstorms from the Northeast and Mid-Atlantic to
the Ohio and Mississippi Valleys Wednesday into Thursday. Locally
heavy rainfall will be possible, especially in the Ohio Valley
where
the front will encounter an anomalously moist air mass and in the
central Appalachians where terrain may enhance precipitation
totals. This frontal boundary should move relatively quickly, which
will help to limit flash flood potential.
The focus for precipitation will shift to the Northwest late this
week into next weekend as upper level troughing strengthens. This
trough will push a cold front south across the Northwest and
northern Plains late this week, which will bring unsettled weather
to these regions. Early next week, the front will continue to move
south across the Great Basin and Intermountain West while low
pressure strengthens along the boundary over the northern/central
Plains. Wintry weather will be possible in the northern Rockies
next weekend as cooler air moves in behind the cold front, and
heavy rainfall potential may develop over eastern Plains into the
Upper Midwest ahead of the strengthening low early next week.
Daily rounds of diurnal showers and thunderstorms are expected to
continue in the Southeast and Southwest. A couple of weak frontal
boundaries will move into the Southeast and dissipate, which will
focus moisture and instability across the region. In the
Southwest, monsoonal moisture will continue focus showers and
thunderstorms in the Four Corners region.
Dolan/Santorelli

Forecaster's Toolbox (Prototype)
Tools Generated at WPC
 These tools are NOT operationally supported. Data may not update regularly due to workstation failure and/or data unavailability.
Experimental Urban Rain Rate Dashboard (URRD)
Visualizes the probability of high rain rates that may lead to flash flooding over the next several days at over 60 major U.S. cities.
Prototype Specialized Excessive Rainfall Maps
Interface for specialized WPC Excessive Rainfall Outlook Maps for NWS County Warning Areas and States.
Local Storm Reports
Custom plots of Local Storm Reports across the Contiguous United States. Reports include rain, snow, ice, and severe weather, as well as other significant information from storm spotters.
Extreme Precipitation Monitor
Displays the climatological significance of precipitation forecast by WPC. The climatological significance
 is represented by Average Recurrence Intervals (ARIs) of precipitation estimates from NOAA Atlas-14 and Atlas2.
Ensemble Situational Awareness Table
An interactive situational awareness table that displays anomalies, percentiles, and return intervals from the GEFS, NAEFS, and ECMWF Ensembles (login required to view ECMWF data). 
*Please note that there is currently an issue where only users on a NOAA network can access this page. We are actively working to resolve this problem. 
NDFD Forecast Temperature Records
Interactive display of where temperatures could approach or exceed records within the contiguous U.S. (based on
 NDFD temperature forecasts)
NWS NDFD Max/Min Temperatures and Departure from Normal
Displays Days 1-7 NDFD maximum and minimum temperatures, along with their respective departures from climatology.
Precipitation Object Tracking
Contains 4 different tools to track precipitation objects (heavy rain and snowfall) from short-range high-resolution deterministic and ensemble guidance. 
Weather in Context Prototype
Displays forecast information and its climatological context to quickly alert a forecaster when a record or neear-record breaking
 event is possible. This tool is available for both CONUS and Alaska.
Automated Forecast Low Clusters
Displays 0-72 hour cyclone forecast positions from global ensemble and deterministic model guidance.
Intense Rainfall and Flash Flood Reports
Displays flood and flash flood reports as well as intense rainfall observations for user-selectable time ranges and customizable geographic regions. 
 Includes ability to download reports and associated metadata in csv format. 
Experimental HeatRisk
The experimental National Weather Service (NWS) HeatRisk is a color-numeric-based index that provides a forecast of the potential level of risk for heat-related impacts to occur over a 24-hour period, with forecasts available out through 7 days.
GEFS Probabilities
Plots of GEFS probabilistic forecast of precipitation, temperature, and sea-level pressure exceeding
 various thresholds.
1/3/6/24-hr Changes
Change in weather parameters (temperature, dewpoint, surface pressure, etc) over the last 1/3/6/24 hours. Data is provided from the Real-Time Mesoscale Analysis (RTMA) or the Rapid Refresh (RAP).
Other Favorite Forecast Tools
CIPS Guidance
Analog guidance that uses an objective approach to find historical events that
 are similar to the upcoming forecast.
National Blend of Models
Nationally consistent and skillful suite of calibrated forecast guidance based on a blend of both NWS and
 non-NWS numerical weather prediction model data and post-processed model guidance.
Atmospheric River Portal
A portal for atmospheric river forecasts and diagnostics from the Center for Western Weather and Water Extremes.
GEFS Plumes
An interactive display of time series plots from GEFS ensemble members at a point.
SPC Forecast Tools
A variety of useful tools generated at The Storm Prediction Center (SPC).
ECMWF Forecast Charts
Output from the ECMWF Integrated Forecasting System (IFS), including medium range deterministic, 
 ensemble, and machine learning model forecasts.
